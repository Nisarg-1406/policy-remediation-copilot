# Investment Policy for Robert and Adaeze Okafor

Prepared by Marcus Hale, CFP, Cedar Ridge Wealth Partners
Agreed with the clients on February 18, 2026

---

This document records how we will manage Robert and Adaeze's savings and what they have asked of us. It replaces the notes from our 2022 onboarding. It covers the three accounts we manage for them at Pinnacle Custody: their joint brokerage account, Robert's IRA and Adaeze's IRA. When we refer to "the portfolio" we mean those three accounts together.

## Where things stand

Robert (67) retired in 2024 after thirty-one years as a signals engineer with Halvorsen Rail. Adaeze (65) retired from nursing administration last year. They own their home outright in Columbus, Ohio, and have no debt apart from a small car loan they expect to pay off in 2027.

Robert receives a monthly pension from the Halvorsen Rail defined-benefit plan. He plans to delay Social Security until 2028, when he turns 70. Adaeze will begin her Social Security benefit in 2027. Until then, the portfolio supplies whatever their pension does not cover.

Both of them describe themselves as careful with money. Robert remembers his parents losing a large part of their savings in the early 1980s, and he has told us several times that a large loss in the years right after retiring is his biggest worry. He is happy to accept a smaller, steadier return to avoid it. Adaeze is somewhat more comfortable with market ups and downs, but she agrees that protecting what they have comes first.

## What they want the money to do

Their first priority is that their savings last. They want the portfolio to pay the gap between their pension and their spending, keep up with inflation over time, and still leave something for their children.

They would also like to help with the education of their three grandchildren. They are thinking about gifts of up to $20,000 a year beginning in 2028, once Robert's Social Security starts. We have agreed to revisit the plan when they decide on the amount.

We have not set a specific return target. Based on the allocation below, we expect long-term returns somewhere in the range of 5% to 6% a year before fees, and they understand this is an estimate.

## How we will invest

Stocks should make up roughly 40% of the portfolio. We will keep them between 30% and 45%, and we will not go outside that range without talking to Robert and Adaeze first. We will hold stocks mainly through broad index funds, with a small number of individual companies. Adaeze prefers companies with a record of paying dividends, and we will lean that way when we choose individual stocks.

At least half of the portfolio should be in bonds. Bonds should be investment grade. Within the bond portfolio, no more than 10% should be in bonds rated in the BBB category. We will favor US Treasuries and high-quality corporate bonds, and we will keep maturities mostly within ten years.

No single stock should be more than 5% of the portfolio.

Because Robert's pension already depends on Halvorsen Rail, they have asked that we never hold Halvorsen Rail stock or bonds in any of their accounts.

## Keeping cash on hand

Robert and Adaeze want to know that a market decline will never force them to sell investments at a bad time to pay their bills. We will keep enough in cash and cash equivalents to cover twelve months of their living expenses, net of Robert's pension. By cash equivalents we mean money market funds and Treasury bills that mature within one year.

We will refresh their expense figures at each annual review, or sooner if they tell us their spending has changed. When Adaeze's Social Security begins in 2027 we will recalculate the amount.

## Taxes and withdrawals

Robert's required minimum distributions from his IRA begin in 2031. Until then, we will generally take withdrawals from the joint account first and use the IRAs for rebalancing, unless their tax preparer advises otherwise. We will send them a withdrawal schedule each December for the following year.

## How we will stay in touch

We will send a written report every quarter and meet in person at least once a year, usually in March after their tax return is filed. Robert and Adaeze have asked us to phone them about anything that needs a decision. Email is fine for everything else. If their health, living situation or spending changes in a meaningful way, they will let us know and we will update this document.
