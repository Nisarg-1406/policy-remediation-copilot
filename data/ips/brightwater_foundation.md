# Brightwater Family Foundation
# Investment Policy Statement

**Approved by the Board of Directors:** June 19, 2026, on the recommendation of the Investment Committee (June 12, 2026)
**Investment advisor:** Cedar Ridge Wealth Partners (the "Advisor")
**Supersedes:** Investment Policy Statement adopted April 22, 2023

---

## 1. Purpose

1.1 The Brightwater Family Foundation (the "Foundation") is a private, non-operating foundation established in 2004 by Thomas and Ilse Varga. Its mission is to protect and restore freshwater ecosystems in the Great Lakes region through grants to conservation organizations, research institutions and community groups.

1.2 This Investment Policy Statement sets out the objectives and guidelines for the investment of the Foundation's assets (the "Portfolio"). It is intended to guide the Investment Committee (the "Committee") and the Advisor, and to support the Committee in meeting its fiduciary duties under applicable law.

1.3 The Portfolio consists of all accounts held in the name of the Foundation and managed by the Advisor, currently the Endowment account and the Grant Reserve account.

## 2. Roles and Responsibilities

2.1 **Board of Directors.** The Board approves this policy and any amendments, approves the annual grant budget, and appoints the members of the Committee.

2.2 **Investment Committee.** The Committee oversees the Portfolio, monitors the Advisor, reviews performance against the objectives in Section 3, and recommends changes to this policy to the Board. The Committee currently consists of Elena Varga (Chair), Samuel Okonjo and Dr. Rachel Stein.

2.3 **Advisor.** The Advisor manages the Portfolio on a discretionary basis within the guidelines in this policy, reports to the Committee quarterly, and notifies the Committee Chair within five business days if the Portfolio falls outside any limit in this policy.

## 3. Investment Objectives

3.1 The primary objective is to preserve and grow the real, inflation-adjusted value of the Portfolio over the long term after grants and expenses, so that the Foundation can support its mission in perpetuity.

3.2 To meet this objective, the Portfolio seeks a long-term total return at least equal to the Foundation's spending rate plus inflation plus investment expenses, which the Committee currently estimates at approximately 8% per year. The Committee recognizes that returns in any given year may be well above or below this level.

3.3 The Foundation has a long time horizon and can tolerate short-term volatility. The Committee places more weight on avoiding a permanent loss of capital than on short-term fluctuations in market value.

## 4. Spending Policy

4.1 The Foundation intends to distribute approximately 5% of the average market value of the Portfolio over the trailing twelve quarters each year. This is intended to meet the minimum distribution requirement for private foundations and to fund its grant program. The Advisor will confirm with the Foundation's accountant each year that the requirement has been met.

4.2 The Board may approve distributions above or below this level in a given year after consulting the Committee.

## 5. Asset Allocation

5.1 The Portfolio will be managed within the following ranges, measured at market value:

| Asset class | Minimum | Target | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global equity | 55% | 65% | 75% |
| Fixed income | 20% | 30% | 40% |
| Cash and cash equivalents | 0% | 5% | 10% |

5.2 The Advisor will rebalance when any asset class moves outside its range and may rebalance within ranges at its discretion.

## 6. Diversification

6.1 The Portfolio should be broadly diversified and avoid excessive concentration in any single sector, industry or theme.

6.2 No single security, other than diversified mutual funds, diversified exchange-traded funds and US Treasury obligations, may exceed 4% of the market value of the Portfolio.

6.3 Fixed income investments should be predominantly investment grade.

## 7. Liquidity

7.1 The Foundation will hold liquid reserves in cash, money market funds or US Treasury bills at least equal to the next twelve months of approved grants.

7.2 The Grant Reserve account is intended to hold these reserves. Reserves held in the Endowment account also count toward this requirement.

## 8. Responsible Investing

8.1 Consistent with its mission, the Foundation will not invest in fossil fuel companies.

8.2 The Committee encourages the Advisor to favor companies with strong water stewardship practices and to report on the Portfolio's exposure to water-intensive industries at least annually.

8.3 The Advisor will vote proxies for individual securities in a manner consistent with the Foundation's mission. The Advisor will provide the Committee with an annual summary of proxy votes on environmental proposals.

## 9. Selection and Review of Investments

9.1 When selecting mutual funds and ETFs, the Advisor will consider investment strategy, long-term performance relative to peers and benchmarks, fees, tracking error for index funds, and the tenure and stability of the management team.

9.2 The Advisor will place any fund that underperforms its benchmark by more than 2% a year over a rolling three-year period on a watch list and report it to the Committee.

## 10. Reporting and Review

10.1 The Committee will meet at least quarterly. The Advisor will provide a written report before each meeting covering performance, asset allocation, compliance with this policy and any recommended changes.

10.2 The Committee will review this policy at least annually and recommend amendments to the Board as needed.
