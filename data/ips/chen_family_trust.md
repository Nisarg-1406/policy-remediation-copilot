# Investment Policy Statement

**Client:** Chen Family Trust u/a dated March 14, 2011, and related accounts of David and Mei Chen
**Prepared by:** Laura Brennan, CFP, Cedar Ridge Wealth Partners (the "Firm")
**Effective date:** November 3, 2025
**Supersedes:** Investment Policy Statement dated December 5, 2022

---

## 1. Purpose

This Investment Policy Statement ("IPS") sets out the objectives, guidelines and restrictions that govern the management of the Portfolio described in Section 2. It is intended to:

- document the Trustees' investment objectives, risk tolerance and constraints;
- give the Firm clear guidelines for managing the Portfolio on a discretionary basis;
- provide a basis for monitoring and evaluating results over time.

This IPS is a statement of policy. It does not guarantee any particular result, and the Trustees understand that the value of the Portfolio will fluctuate.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This IPS governs the following accounts, which together make up the "Portfolio":

| Account | Registration | Custodian |
|---|---|---|
| Trust investment account | Chen Family Trust u/a 2011 | Pinnacle Custody |
| Individual account | David Chen | Pinnacle Custody |
| Roth IRA | Mei Chen | Pinnacle Custody |

2.2 David's employer 401(k) plan, held at Ashbury Retirement Services, is not managed by the Firm. The Firm reviews it annually for planning purposes and may offer recommendations. It is not part of the Portfolio and is not subject to the limits in Sections 4 through 7. Percentages in this IPS are measured against the market value of the Portfolio only.

2.3 The Trustees are David Chen and Mei Chen. Either Trustee may act alone in communicating with the Firm on matters covered by this IPS.

## 3. Background and Objectives

3.1 **Background.** David (56) is an orthopedic surgeon in private practice and expects to work until about age 65. Mei (54) is a partner at an architecture firm. The Trust was established in 2011 for the benefit of their two children, now 22 and 19, and is intended to provide for their education and early adult life and to pass remaining assets to them over time. David and Mei's personal accounts are part of their own retirement savings.

3.2 **Return objective.** The long-term objective is to earn a total return, net of fees, that exceeds the rate of inflation (CPI-U) by 3% per year, measured over rolling ten-year periods. This is a long-term goal. The Firm does not guarantee it or any minimum annual return.

3.3 **Risk tolerance.** The Trustees have a moderate tolerance for risk. They are prepared to accept declines in market value in some years in exchange for long-term growth, but they experienced significant stress during the 2008 and 2022 market declines and have asked that the Portfolio be managed to avoid drawdowns much larger than those of a balanced 60/40 portfolio.

3.4 **Time horizon.** The Portfolio has a time horizon of more than fifteen years. The Trust expects to make distributions to beneficiaries for at least the next ten years.

3.5 **Liquidity.** The Trust currently distributes approximately $150,000 to beneficiaries each January for tuition and living costs. The Firm will plan for this distribution through interest and dividend income and year-end rebalancing. The Trustees do not anticipate other significant withdrawals.

## 4. Asset Allocation

4.1 The Portfolio will be managed within the following ranges, measured at market value:

| Asset class | Minimum | Target | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equity (US and international) | 50% | 60% | 70% |
| Fixed income | 25% | 35% | 45% |
| Cash and cash equivalents | 0% | 5% | 10% |
| Alternative investments | 0% | 0% | 10% |

4.2 International equity will represent between 10% and 20% of the market value of the Portfolio.

4.3 For purposes of the table in 4.1, a multi-asset or balanced fund is classified according to its predominant asset class.

4.4 The targets reflect the Trustees' long-term preferences. The Firm may operate anywhere within the ranges based on market conditions and its judgment.

## 5. Diversification and Concentration

5.1 **Single issuer.** Exposure to any single issuer shall not exceed 5% of the market value of the Portfolio. Exposure to an issuer includes all of its securities held in the Portfolio, including every class of common or preferred stock and any bonds, as well as indirect exposure through mutual funds and exchange-traded funds held in the Portfolio. Obligations of the United States Treasury and US government agencies are exempt from this limit.

5.2 **Pooled vehicles.** No single mutual fund or exchange-traded fund shall exceed 15% of the market value of the Portfolio, except for the Portfolio's core US broad-market index fund, which may represent up to 30%.

5.3 The Firm will monitor the Portfolio for overlap between funds and individual holdings and will consider that overlap when adding new positions.

## 6. Fixed Income Guidelines

6.1 Individual bonds must be rated A- or better by S&P or A3 or better by Moody's.

6.2 Bond funds must invest primarily in investment-grade securities.

6.3 The Firm will aim to keep the average duration of the fixed income allocation within two years of the duration of the Bloomberg US Aggregate Bond Index.

6.4 Where practical, taxable bonds will be held in the Roth IRA and the Trust account, and the individual account will favor tax-efficient holdings.

## 7. Risk Management

7.1 **Equity ceiling.** In light of the Trustees' experience in 2008 and 2022, total equity exposure, including the equity portion of balanced and multi-asset funds, shall not exceed 60% of the market value of the Portfolio.

7.2 **Prohibited transactions.** The Portfolio will not use leverage or margin, sell securities short, or hold derivatives, other than derivatives held within diversified mutual funds or ETFs as part of their normal management.

7.3 **Illiquid investments.** The Portfolio will not hold private placements, private funds, limited partnership interests that do not trade on a public exchange, or other investments that cannot ordinarily be sold within five business days.

## 8. Rebalancing

8.1 The Firm will review the Portfolio's allocation at least quarterly.

8.2 When an asset class falls outside its range in Section 4.1, the Firm will rebalance to within the range within 30 days, taking into account tax consequences and transaction costs.

8.3 The Firm may use cash flows, including the annual Trust distribution, to rebalance.

## 9. Tax Considerations

9.1 The Firm will consider the tax consequences of transactions in the individual and Trust accounts, including the realization of capital gains, and will harvest losses where it is practical and consistent with the Portfolio's objectives.

9.2 The Trustees' tax advisor is Gregory Lim, CPA, of Lim & Associates. The Firm will coordinate with him before the end of each calendar year.

## 10. Review and Communication

10.1 The Firm will provide quarterly performance reports for each account and for the Portfolio as a whole.

10.2 The Firm and the Trustees will meet at least once a year to review the Portfolio, the Trustees' circumstances and this IPS.

10.3 The Trustees will inform the Firm promptly of any material change in their financial situation, objectives or the needs of the beneficiaries.

10.4 This IPS will be reviewed at least every three years, or sooner if the Trustees' circumstances change.
